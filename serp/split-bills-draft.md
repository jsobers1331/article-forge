# 
          How to Split Bills With a Roommate Without a Spreadsheet
        

        

          Stop using spreadsheets for roommate bill splitting. A dedicated household bill tracker
          logs each bill once, auto-converts currencies if needed, shows who owes what, and
          tracks settling up — without linking a bank account or maintaining a formula. You enter
          the bill amount, pick who paid, and the app tracks the balance and payment status. No
          tabs, no formula errors, no group-chat math.
        

        
          
        

        
## Why is a spreadsheet so bad for splitting bills?

        

          Because it was never built for this job. A spreadsheet is a static snapshot — someone
          has to remember to open it, update it, redo the math every time a bill changes. It
          doesn't flag a bill as due. It doesn't notice when a payment's late. It
          just sits there, blank and patient, until somebody finally does the arithmetic by hand.
        

        

          You're not bad at spreadsheets. The tool is just wrong for recurring shared
          expenses. Your roommate covers internet one month, you cover electricity the next, and
          within a few weeks "who owes whom" has turned into a small archaeology project.
          Cross-currency households have it worse — now you're manually looking up an
          exchange rate and hoping the number still holds by the time you settle up.
        

        
## What should I use instead of a spreadsheet?

        

          Three things matter more than anything else: manual entry (not a budgeting app that
          wants your bank login), per-bill split logic, and payment-status tracking. Everything
          else is a nice-to-have.
        

        
1. **Manual entry only.** You type in the bill amount and due date — no bank sync, no read access to your checking account.
2. **Per-bill split logic.** Say "this bill is 50/50" or "this one's on me" and have the app track the resulting balance.
3. **Payment status tracking.** See whether a bill has been paid, who paid it, and who still owes.
4. **Multi-currency support.** If you and your roommate earn or pay in different currencies, amounts should auto-convert.
5. **Export capability.** Get your data out as CSV any time, without a paywall.

        

          Where does everyone else land on this? 
          [Splitwise](https://splitwise.com) 
          genuinely wins on simplicity for a pure IOU — "I paid for dinner, you owe me
          $20." It just doesn't track recurring bills with due dates, and it has no
          opinion on whether your household is paying on time. 
          [YNAB](https://ynab.com), 
          [Monarch](https://monarchmoney.com),
          and [Copilot](https://copilot.money) 
          are excellent budgeting apps built for one person's finances, not a shared
          household — and they all want bank access, which is exactly what you're trying to
          avoid here. Fine tools. Wrong job.

6. **Settle up.** The app shows the net balance; you settle in cash, Venmo, Zelle, or whatever method you prefer. It doesn't process the payment itself, just tells you who owes what.

        

          That's the whole loop. It takes less time than opening the spreadsheet did.
        

        
## What's the difference between a bill tracker and a budgeting app?

        

          One question each. A bill tracker asks: are our shared bills paid on time and split
          fairly? A budgeting app asks: am I spending too much on groceries? Related questions,
          but not the same one — and answering the second doesn't help with the first.
        

        

          HomeWeal only answers the first question, on purpose. No bank login, no categorizing
          your personal purchases, no gamified savings streaks. It tracks what you enter, converts
          currency where it needs to, and tells you who owes what. That narrowness is a privacy
          feature if you're living with a roommate rather than a partner — your personal
          spending simply isn't in scope.
        

        
## Common mistakes when splitting bills with a roommate

        
          
- **Using a shared card without a rule.** If bills go on one card, agree explicitly who pays the statement and when the other reimburses.
          
- **Ignoring due dates.** A late bill costs more (late fees, interest) — the Health Score exists to catch this before it happens.
          
- **Mixing personal and shared expenses.** Keep the household tracker strictly for shared bills, not personal subscriptions.
          
- **Assuming 50/50 is always fair.** If income or usage differs meaningfully, a custom split may be fairer — the app supports it.
          
- **Waiting until month-end to reconcile.** Log bills as they arrive; real-time entry beats a monthly catch-up session.
        

        
## Who this isn't for

        

          Living alone, or sharing a fully joint account with a partner and not tracking
          individual contributions? Just pay the bills — you don't need this. Want a full
          budgeting tool that forecasts retirement savings and categorizes every purchase? YNAB or
          Monarch genuinely do that better. Splitting a one-off dinner with friends rather than a
          recurring bill? Use Splitwise — free, simple, built for exactly that. HomeWeal is for
          the bill that comes back every month, not the restaurant tab.
