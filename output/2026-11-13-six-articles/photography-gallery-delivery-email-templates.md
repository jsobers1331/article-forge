## What These Templates Include—and Exclude

The four templates cover the most common gallery delivery moments:

1. Notifying a client their photographs are ready.
2. Requesting favorites or a final selection.
3. Reminding a client before an agreed gallery access deadline.
4. Assisting a client who cannot access the gallery or download files.

Set deadlines and access instructions from the agreement for that specific job. If you use ShootMuse, the [galleries feature](/features/galleries) provides branded client galleries with password protection and favorites, but you are responsible for composing and sending emails. The templates are neutral and suitable for any gallery host, direct file transfer, or client portal.

**Important:** Keep access codes, passwords, or private gallery links in the secure channel you and your client agreed on. Do not include a password in the same email as the gallery link unless that is your established method. The access help template also reminds clients not to send passwords or payment details by email.

## Template 1: First Delivery

Use this template when the gallery is ready and the client has not seen the work yet. The goal is to provide clear instructions and get the client into the gallery efficiently.

> **Subject:** Your photographs are ready
>
> Hello,
>
> Your photographs are ready. Use the private gallery link included with this message. Please read the access and download instructions below before sharing the link. Reply if you need help.

**How to adapt:**
- Replace the second sentence to match your actual deliverable (e.g., full-resolution downloads or web-size proofs).
- Add specific access and download instructions as needed.

**Before sending:**
- Test the gallery link as a recipient (use a private/incognito window or a secondary email address).
- Confirm the gallery displays only the intended client’s work and job.

## Template 2: Selection Request

Send this when the client needs to choose favorites, mark images for retouching, or approve a final set. Reference your agreed selection method without restating it in full.

> **Subject:** Please review your gallery and send your selections
>
> Hello,
>
> Please review your gallery and send your choices using the selection method we agreed. Let me know when your selection is final so I can confirm the next step.

**How to adapt:**
- If you have not agreed on a selection method, clarify this with the client first.
- If your process includes a purchase or approval step, add a sentence explaining what happens after selection.

**Tip:** Separating favorites from purchasing or final approval avoids confusion. Marking favorites is not the same as placing an order or approving a final set.

## Template 3: Access Reminder Before an Agreed Deadline

Use this only if you and the client have already set a gallery access deadline. This template does not create a deadline or claim the gallery will expire automatically.

> **Subject:** Reminder: your gallery access deadline is approaching
>
> Hello,
>
> This is a reminder of the gallery access deadline we already agreed. Please save the included files before that date. If you need help, reply before the deadline so we can discuss the options.

**How to adapt:**
- Replace “the included files” with the actual deliverable (e.g., full-resolution images, proofing gallery, print selection set).
- If the client only needs to make selections, adjust the reminder accordingly.

## Template 4: Access Help

Send this when a client reports issues accessing the gallery, entering a password, or downloading files. The aim is to gather enough information to diagnose the problem without requesting sensitive details.

> **Subject:** Let's fix your gallery access
>
> Hello,
>
> Thanks for letting me know. Please confirm which step stops working and the error message you see. Please do not send a password or private payment details. I will check the link and reply with the next step.

**How to adapt:**
- Ask for the specific step and any error messages.
- Remind clients not to send passwords or payment details by email.

**After receiving a reply:**
- Check the gallery link yourself to confirm it is active and the access code works.
- Verify the recipient’s email address matches your records.
- If your gallery platform provides access logs, check for client attempts.
- Respond with one clear next step.

## Pre-Send Checklist for Gallery Delivery Emails

Before sending any gallery delivery email, use this checklist to avoid common mistakes:

| Check        | What to confirm                                                                 |
|--------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Link         | Open the gallery link in a private window as a recipient would.                  |
| Recipient    | Confirm the email address belongs to the intended client.                        |
| Privacy      | Confirm the gallery shows only that client's work, not another job.              |
| Access code  | Confirm the password or code is in the agreed secure channel, not in the email.  |
| Downloads    | Confirm download permissions match what you told the client.                     |
| Deliverables | Confirm the email describes the actual files, resolution, and quantity.          |
| Deadline     | If you mention a deadline, confirm it matches the one already agreed.            |

This checklist is a proposed process, not a universal standard. Adapt it to your platform, contract terms, and client relationship. The goal is to verify all details before the client receives the message.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Including both password and link in the same email without prior agreement.** If intercepted or forwarded, the gallery could be accessed by unintended recipients. Use your established secure channel for access codes.
- **Inventing an expiration date.** Only mention deadlines you have agreed to with the client. If you want a deadline, agree to it first.
- **Promising access you have not agreed to provide.** Confirm the access window and download permissions before describing them in the message.
- **Treating favorites as a purchase or final approval.** Marking favorites is not an order or approval; clarify the next step.
- **Sending the same email for every job.** Adapt instructions for each type of project (e.g., weddings, headshots, commercial work).
