## Image acceptance evidence

The first candidates were rejected: empty wedding chairs, wedding accessories, people reviewing blank paper, and generic home scenes did not show the reader's task. The photography-site replacements use first-party photos `jsobers/wedding/71666891` (a posed wedding group) and `jsobers/wedding/950999913` (dress detail). Their alt text describes visible content without inventing a family relationship, venue, or date. The four business and household replacements are explanatory graphics whose wording and illustrative arithmetic were checked against the articles. Three initial graphics failed the perceptual duplicate check because they shared a layout; redesigned versions now pass.

All six landscape assets are decodable WebP images at least 1536 pixels wide, with descriptive alt text and passing duplicate scans. The four explanatory graphics also have 1200×1600 portrait variants; browser checks at 390px confirmed each mobile source is selected, readable, and does not cause horizontal overflow. The `images/*.quality.json` receipts record article-specific fit and provenance. Local site builds verified each hero, OG image, Article schema image, and sitemap image reference. Production placement and crawlability remain unverified until publication.

## Review findings and next improvements

- **Wedding family photo list — 83.** A named-group worksheet, two-gatherer plan, and destination-guest check give the draft a concrete job. The existing [family session guide](https://www.jsobersphotography.com/blog/family-photographer-barbados) addresses visiting families, not wedding group coordination. The [published wedding collections](https://www.jsobersphotography.com/wedding-packages) support the coverage-hour discussion. Evidence is limited because the worksheet is an editorial example, not a documented real wedding; a permissioned sample from a past booking would strengthen it.
- **Getting-ready timeline — 80.** The backward-planning table makes preparation coverage a specific decision and explains its trade-off against the [published four-, six-, and eight-hour collections](https://www.jsobersphotography.com/wedding-packages). It is narrower than the site's [booking-from-abroad guide](https://www.jsobersphotography.com/blog/how-to-book-a-barbados-wedding-photographer-from-abroad). The schedule deliberately avoids invented exact times; a reviewed timeline from a real venue and date would raise usefulness and evidence.
- **Invoice checklist — 78.** The draft covers the booking-to-payment sequence but lacks a filled sample invoice or reusable template. [Adobe's photography invoicing guide](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat/business/hub/how-to-create-invoice-for-photography.html) illustrates the level of payment-term and deposit detail readers may expect. Reconcile product-specific wording with [ShootMuse pricing](https://shootmuse.com/pricing) and [invoicing](https://shootmuse.com/features/invoicing) before publication.
- **Invoice reminder templates — 82.** Three copyable messages and a state/stop-rule table make the purpose distinct from the general invoice checklist and from ShootMuse's broad [workflow-automation guide](https://shootmuse.com/blog/photography-workflow-automation). The templates still need a test against an actual invoice and mobile email rendering; no first-hand outcome is claimed. [ShootMuse's invoicing page](https://shootmuse.com/features/invoicing) supports the capability and [pricing](https://shootmuse.com/pricing) establishes plan boundaries.
- **Annual household bills — 85.** A reserve calculation and payday review add a concrete method beyond HomeWeal's [monthly planner article](https://homeweal.com/articles/what-is-a-good-monthly-planner-for-bills). The [CFPB recommends accounting for less-frequent expenses](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/prepare/assess-your-spending/), while [HomeWeal documents yearly bill schedules](https://homeweal.com/features). A worked multi-bill worksheet would improve it further.
- **Variable utility bills — 81.** The estimate-versus-confirmed method is useful, though HomeWeal already mentions variable amounts in other articles. The [CFPB bill-calendar source](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/blog/budget-help-manage-your-monthly-expenses-bill-calendar/) supports recording due dates and amounts, but does not validate every estimation method. A utility-provider source or worked bills would improve its evidence score.
